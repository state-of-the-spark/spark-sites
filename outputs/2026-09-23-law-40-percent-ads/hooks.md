# Law Firm Ad Hooks (Meta, B2B, Florida)

40 hooks for Meta ads targeting FLORIDA LAW FIRM OWNERS AND ATTORNEYS (solo and small
practices, not consumers looking for a lawyer). Sourced entirely from
`spark-sites/outputs/2026-09-23-law-40-percent-report/the-40-percent-problem.md`
("the report" in citations below). Every hook that makes a factual claim is annotated
with the source. Hooks are the first line only, not the offer and not the CTA.
Numbered H01-H40 for reference in an ad matrix.

**A compliance note before the list.** Meta's personal-attributes policy gets stricter
scrutiny on legal-adjacent ads. The roofer precedent used direct "you/your" address
freely ("How many roofs do you have to sell..."). For this audience, "you" is riskier
because it can read as asserting a personal attribute about the reader ("Are you a
lawyer who...?" implies you know the viewer's profession, which Meta can reject).
The safer, and equally strong, move is to talk about the FIRM in the third person
("Florida law firms...", "a small firm's front desk...") or address "your firm" /
"your website" as a business asset rather than the person's identity. All 40 hooks
below are written that way; none opens with a personal "you are a..." construction.

## 1. Loss / what it is costing right now

**H01.** Six in ten law firms across the country don't pick up the phone when a new client calls. (Truth #1: 40% answered in 2024, down from 56% in 2019)

**H02.** A $16.73 click on "criminal defense lawyer near me" turns into a $239 lead, and then the phone rings out. (Truth #2: CPC + ~7% click-to-inquiry rate)

**H03.** A missed call at 12:15 can cost a Florida firm a signed client by 2:00 the same afternoon. (Truth #1: the scared-caller behavior described in the report)

**H04.** Every contact form that sits unread overnight is a paid click a firm already bought and let go cold. (Truth #2)

**H05.** One firm paid more than $28,000 on a $2,336-a-month contract and never got the website it was promised. (Truth #5: the FindLaw/Thomson Reuters lawsuit)

## Strongest 6

Six chosen for spread across registers, not six versions of the same appeal (method
explained fully in `README.md`):

1. **H26**: self-audit question, the report's own central math, no setup required.
2. **H18**: hard number, indicts the exact thing a firm is already paying for (an expensive click that goes nowhere).
3. **H31**: enemy naming, ownership anxiety about who actually controls the firm's own assets.
4. **H03**: visceral specific, the scared-caller-hires-someone-else scenario in one sentence.
5. **H13**: curiosity gap, an underused Bar-compliant lever most firms have never touched.
6. **H28**: direct ownership question, same axis as H31 but framed as a question rather than a statement, useful for testing register against register.
