## Why this exists

On our call I promised you a roadmap that plugs the holes in the ship, so every bit of work you're already doing starts showing up in the numbers.

That matters for two reasons. First, it makes the marketing you do from here forward count twice: once when someone sees it, and again when you retarget them later. Second, any partner worth talking to (us, your SCORE mentor's network, or someone else) is going to ask to see a pulse: traffic, social activity, sales history. This roadmap gets you that pulse in about 30 days, and it gets you into those conversations knowing what's fair.

## A quick correction from our call

On the call I said I didn't see Google Analytics on your site. I looked again, more carefully, and **you do have Google Analytics 4 installed and collecting data** (measurement ID G-TNGHVFG5Q6). Good news.

What's actually missing is the retargeting layer:

- **No Meta (Facebook/Instagram) Pixel.** Nobody who has visited your site can be shown an ad on Facebook or Instagram later, and Meta can't learn who your buyers are.
- **No Google Ads conversion or remarketing tag.** Same gap on the Google side: no way to follow up with past visitors on YouTube, Gmail, or search.

So the point stands: years of traffic have come and gone without being cookied for retargeting. The fix is quick.

## The Level 1 Playbook: five moves, about 30 days

Do these in order. Moves 1 and 2 matter most, because they make every later dollar and every later post work harder.

### Move 1: Turn on the tracking (week 1)

- Install the **Meta Pixel plus Conversions API**. The free "Facebook for WooCommerce" plugin does both.
- Install the **Google Ads conversion and remarketing tag**. The free "Google for WooCommerce" plugin does this and also puts your products into free Google Shopping listings.
- Confirm GA4 is recording **view item, add to cart and purchase** events.
- Confirm **you personally have admin access** to GA4, your Google Business Profile, a Meta Business account, and your domain. If your web support company set these up, make sure the keys are in your name too.

**Done when:** a $1 test purchase shows up in both GA4 and Meta Events Manager.

### Move 2: Make the $699 page close the sale (weeks 1-2)

- **Get 5 to 10 reviews from past buyers.** Email or call them personally (your strength). Post them on the product page and ask the happiest ones for a Google review too.
- **Put a 60-90 second demo video at the top.** Land and water, setup, a few moves.
- **Add a trust block next to Add to Cart:** warranty, shipping, the 30-day returns you already offer, "patented design," and your phone number.
- **Offer a payment plan.** Stripe supports Klarna and Affirm. "$699, or pay monthly" lowers the bar for a retiree on a fixed income.
- **Finish the details:** move it out of "Uncategorized," and add height range, weight capacity, materials, and whether it's saltwater-safe.

**Done when:** a stranger can land on that page and get every answer without calling you.

### Move 3: Catch the people who aren't ready yet (week 2)

- **Turn "The 15-Minute Launchpad" into a free workout offer.** You already filmed it on October 2. Add move names on screen and a short voiceover, and it becomes the reason people give you their email.
- **Use HubSpot (already installed) for the sign-up form and a 5-email welcome series:** your story and your mother's prototype, how the Frame works, the four points of contact, real customers, and then the offer.

**Done when:** you sign up with your own email and the first message lands within a minute.

### Move 4: Clean the storefront (weeks 2-3)

- Remove the COVID statement from the homepage carousel.
- Give the homepage one clear headline that names the product and who it's for.
- Write page descriptions and a share image so links look right in Google, texts and Facebook.
- Fix the four accessibility basics: allow zoom on phones, show a focus outline for keyboard users, label the social icons, darken the low-contrast text.
- Point the "Reviews" menu item at real reviews on your own site instead of a dead link.
- Slim the homepage. Product-checkout scripts are loading on every page and they don't need to.

**Done when:** a free accessibility scan comes back clean on the basics, and the homepage loads in a few seconds on a phone.

### Move 5: Start the pulse (weeks 1-4, then ongoing)

- **Open a branded YouTube channel** ("Beach Athletic" or "The Fitness Frame") separate from your personal one.
- **Rhythm:** 1 long video a week plus 3 Shorts a week, cut from your Drive library. Post the same Shorts to Instagram Reels, Facebook and TikTok.
- **The editing shortcut we talked about:** drop raw footage in a folder, have Claude work with Descript to cut it, and give it a few reference videos for your style.
- **Minimum bar:** 3 posts a week. Consistency beats polish.
- **Film the Pull Up Challenge and Dead Hang event.** Faces, reactions, a leaderboard. That's a month of content from one afternoon.

**Done when:** you've posted for 4 straight weeks without missing.

## Your Partnership Readiness Pack

These are the things any marketing partner (equity, commission or plain fee) is going to ask for. Having them in one folder puts you in the driver's seat.

1. **Sales history:** the last 18 months by month, units sold, and where each sale came from (website, events, retail, word of mouth).
2. **Unit economics:** what one Frame costs you landed, your shipping cost, and your gross margin per Frame. This is the number every commission conversation starts from.
3. **Supply:** inventory on hand, reorder lead time, and how many units a month you could ship if demand tripled.
4. **Returns and warranty claims:** rate and reasons.
5. **Traffic and audience baseline:** the GA4 last 90 days, social follower counts, email list size. (After Move 1, this becomes a real number.)
6. **Intellectual property:** patent and trademark numbers and filing dates.
7. **Ownership:** who owns what today, and any existing agreements.
8. **Your role:** a one-paragraph answer to "what do I keep doing, and what do I hand off?"
9. **Your offer:** what you're willing to give (fee, commission per unit, equity) and what you expect it to buy.

## The influencer and affiliate starter kit

Your plan (gift a Frame, pay commission on sales) is the right one. To run it without getting burned:

- **Set up an affiliate program** inside WooCommerce, so each creator gets a unique link and code and sales are tracked automatically.
- **Pick a commission.** For a $699 product, 10% to 15% ($70 to $105 a unit) is a common starting range. Decide it after you know your margin per Frame (Readiness Pack #2).
- **Put it in writing:** posting requirements, FTC disclosure, and lifetime rights for you to reuse everything they film.
- **Start with 10 micro-creators** (roughly 5,000 to 100,000 followers) in senior fitness, aquatic fitness, physical therapy and pool life. They tend to get higher engagement than the big names and cost far less.
- **Keep a dream list** for later, like the Granny Guns and Fit Old Boy caliber we talked about.

## Before the next big channel bet

Pinch A Penny taught an expensive lesson: a year and about $20,000 to find out pool owners don't want to exercise in their pools. Today you can test a channel for a few hundred dollars in a few weeks before committing a year to it.

Your outside-sales background points at channels where institutions buy, and where one "yes" can mean several units and a room full of users:

- Physical therapy and aquatic therapy clinics
- YMCAs, aquatic centers and community pools
- 55+ communities and senior living (The Villages is in your backyard)
- Resorts, hotels and condo associations with pools

The test: a one-page offer for one segment, a short list of 25 targets, your calls plus a small retargeting budget, and 30 days to see who bites. Then scale what works.

## Questions to bring to your SCORE mentor

1. Based on my margin per Frame, what commission per unit can I afford to pay a partner or a creator?
2. Does it make sense to offer equity at all at this stage, or start with commission and earn into equity?
3. If equity, what's a sane ceiling, what vesting schedule, and what protects me if it doesn't work out?
4. Which one segment should I prove first: direct to consumers online, or institutions through outside sales?
5. Is my business set up (entity, IP ownership, books) for a partner or a buyer to do diligence?
