## Four ways to structure the deal

These are illustrations to start the conversation, not a contract. Run every one past your SCORE mentor, and have an attorney paper anything involving equity.

### A. Straight fee

You pay the tier price. You keep 100% of the upside and the company.

- **Best when:** you have the cash flow and want to stay the sole owner.
- **Watch for:** make sure the partner reports results monthly and that every account is owned in your name.

### B. Reduced fee plus commission per Frame

The partner takes a lower monthly fee in exchange for a cut of each Frame they can prove they sold (through a tracked link, code or ad). Their cut grows as volume grows.

**Example on Tier 2:**

- Monthly fee drops from $3,250 to $1,950.
- $60 per attributed Frame for the first 20 each month.
- $75 per Frame for 21-50.
- $90 per Frame above 50.

**What it looks like at 30 attributed Frames a month:**

- Revenue: 30 × $699 = $20,970
- Commission: (20 × $60) + (10 × $75) = $1,950
- Total partner cost: $1,950 fee + $1,950 commission = $3,900, about 19% of that revenue.

- **Best when:** you want the partner to have skin in the game without giving up ownership.
- **Watch for:** this only works if tracking is solid. That's why Move 1 of your roadmap comes first.

### C. Commission only

No monthly fee. The partner earns only on sales, typically 15% to 25% of attributed revenue.

- **Best when:** a partner believes in the product enough to carry all the risk.
- **Watch for:** they'll usually ask for exclusivity and for 12-18 months of financials before they say yes, and they'll favor quick sales over brand building. This is the "they want to see a pulse first" conversation from our call.

### D. The stair-step into equity (what we talked about)

Start small, prove it, then earn ownership in steps.

| Stage | Timing | Structure | Unlocks when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial | Months 1-3 | Option B (fee + commission) | Tracking live, reporting monthly, first sales attributed |
| Proving | Months 4-6 | Option B with a higher commission tier | Averaging an agreed target, e.g. 25 attributed Frames a month |
| Partner | Month 7+ | An equity grant, e.g. 2%, vesting over 4 years with a 1-year cliff | Sustaining the target for 3 straight months |
| Growth | Year 2+ | Further grants at bigger milestones, capped (e.g. 5% total) | e.g. 50+ Frames a month, a retail or TV deal |

**Protections to build in:**

- The intellectual property stays in your company.
- Unvested equity goes away if the partner leaves or misses targets.
- You keep a right to buy back vested shares.
- Every ad, social and email account is owned by the company, not the partner.
- If the company is sold, everyone's vesting speeds up, so the partner is motivated to build toward an exit.

- **Best when:** you want someone thinking like an owner for the next 2-4 years, the way we described the Famous Amos and Colonel Sanders model.
- **Watch for:** equity is the most expensive currency you have. Spend it only after someone has proven themselves.

## How to shop this

I meant it on the call: have five to ten more of these conversations before you decide. Here's how to get real comparisons instead of sales pitches.

**The pitch to give each firm, in your words:**

"I'm Tony Salomone. I invented The Fitness Frame, a patented amphibious training frame for land and water, $699, aimed at baby boomers. I create the content. I'm looking for a marketing partner to handle digital, ads, social, email and creators. I've had a few quotes. Tell me what you'd do in the first 90 days, what it costs, and whether you'd consider a fee-plus-commission or equity arrangement."

**Questions to ask every one of them:**

1. What would you do first, and why?
2. What exactly will I see every month, and what numbers will you be accountable for?
3. Who owns the ad accounts, the pixel, the email list and the content if we part ways? (The answer should be: you do.)
4. Would you take a lower fee for a commission per unit? At what numbers?
5. Have you ever worked for equity? How did it vest?
6. Who on your team actually does the work?
7. Can I talk to a client who sells a physical product?

Then line their answers up against this document.
