Spark Intake · design spec for law firms

The first reply should feel like a receptionist, not a receipt.

Four moments between "nobody picked up" and "consultation booked." Each one is sent in the firm's name, from the firm's own local number, in the firm's own letterhead. None of them ever discuss the case. The firm approves every word once, at setup.

Not built yet. Our pages promise this today, but no system sends it for any client. The mockups below are the target; the build options are at the bottom. The firm shown, "Harbor & Pine Law," is fictional.

1. Missed call → text within a minute

The caller hangs up and, before they dial the next firm on the list, their phone buzzes with a text from the number they just called. That's the whole trick: it's not a stranger, it's the firm.

  • From the firm's own local number, signed with the firm name. No short codes, no links in the first text.
  • Asks only what kind of matter and when to call. Never "tell us what happened."
  • After hours it says when the office opens. Criminal defense firms can add their after-hours line for arrests.

2. Consultation request, not a contact form

This replaces the Gravity Forms look: one short card, three quick taps, and a clear "what happens next." It still feeds the same inbox and alert.

  • Taps, not typing: matter type, county, best time. Name and mobile last.
  • A plain confidentiality line: don't include case details; this doesn't create an attorney-client relationship.
  • The confirmation shows the timeline and an attorney's face, so it feels like a person is on it.

3. The instant reply, on letterhead

A text within the minute, plus this email. It looks like it came from the firm's front desk, because the firm wrote it.

  • Firm letterhead, the attorney's name, office address. No marketing banner.
  • Two or three real consultation times they can tap, or "we'll call you."
  • Repeats the confidentiality line. Nothing about the case.

4. The firm's alert: one card, one tap to call

The firm's phone gets a clean lead card in the app, by text, or both. Whoever is on callback duty taps once to call from the firm's number. When the call happens, it's logged, and that's also how we know the "first new-client inquiry" has arrived for billing.

  • Shows what was already said to the person, so nobody repeats themselves.
  • Conflict check stays with the firm: name first, details on the call.
  • If nobody marks it contacted within the firm's window, it nudges again.

How we'd build it

Two ways to make this real

Recommended

GoHighLevel, one sub-account per firm

  • Missed-call text-back is built in, plus forms, booking calendars, a two-way text inbox, and a mobile app for the alert card.
  • Unlimited plan is $297/mo for unlimited client sub-accounts; texts run under a cent each.
  • We style the form and emails to the firm's letterhead, as above.
  • Each firm's number needs US texting (A2P 10DLC) registration, which takes days to a few weeks. Start at signing.
Custom

Twilio + n8n + our own form

  • Cheapest per firm and fully ours: Twilio number with call forwarding, n8n sends the text and the alert, a custom form on the firm's WordPress site.
  • More build and more upkeep: we'd be writing the inbox, the alert, and the booking ourselves.
  • Same A2P registration requirement.
