# Roofing Owner Ad Hooks (Meta, B2B, Central Florida)

40 hooks for Meta ads targeting roofing COMPANY OWNERS (not homeowners) in Central Florida. Sourced from:

1. `research/2026-09-09-roofing-dream-buyer-profile.md` — the merged dream buyer profile (9 answers, Facebook scan, vernacular)
2. `research/2026-09-09-roofing-dream-buyer-desk-research.md` — the 72-source desk research behind that profile
3. `spark-sites/outputs/2026-09-11-roofer-report/what-your-marketing-company-wont-tell-a-florida-roofer.md` — the client-facing report ("the report" in citations below)

Every hook that makes a factual claim is annotated with the source it rests on. Hooks are the first line only, not the offer and not the CTA. Numbered H01-H40 for reference in an ad matrix.

**H01.** That $3,000-a-month agency bill is costing you a full roof's revenue, more than once a year. (Report Truth #2: $3,000-5,000/mo retainer against an $18,000-32,000 FL roof = 1.1 to 3.3 roofs of revenue a year)

**H02.** That lead you just paid for already went out to as many as 16 other roofers. (Report Truth #1: shared Angi-style lead sold to up to 16 contractors)

**H03.** Nobody is tracking the calls your own website is losing you right now. (Report Truth #3: standard analytics doesn't count a phone-number tap by default)

**H04.** Every hour your web form sits unread, the next storm's biggest job walks to someone else. (Report Truth #5: 84% hang up on unknown numbers, average email reply is 90 minutes)

**H05.** Right now, some other roofer in your town owns the map pack that should be yours. (Report Truth #4: Google shows a short local list, usually three businesses, above everything else)

## Strongest 6

Six I would test first against a cold audience of roofing owners:

1. **H26** — "How many roofs do you have to sell just to pay your marketing company back?" This is the report's own central gut-check question. It works on anyone currently paying an agency, needs no setup, and forces a number into his head before he's even clicked.
2. **H01** — the $3,000-a-month-equals-a-roof math. Owners think in roofs and squares, not marketing jargon. Converting an invoice into "a roof's worth of revenue" is the single most native-to-his-brain reframe in the whole report.
3. **H16** — the $228/$600-1,200 stat. Hard numbers stop a scroll better than adjectives, and this one indicts the exact thing he's currently buying (a shared lead) with a real, sourced figure.
4. **H32** — "Storm chasers aren't beating you on the roof. They're beating you online." Taps identity (local pride, being the trusted name) and a live fear (storm chasers) at once. Contrarian enough to make him stop and defend himself.
5. **H13** — the SB 808 curiosity hook. It's timely (the rule took effect this year), exclusive-feeling ("most agencies haven't touched it"), and gives him something to act on this storm season, not someday.
6. **H39** — the Superior Spray 276%-in-90-days proof point. Cold audiences that don't respond to fear or math often respond to a concrete, third-party result. It's the one hook here built for the skeptic, not the frustrated owner.
